# Trip Credits & Paywall — Product and Technical Spec

**Status:** Draft for review · **Owner:** Meera · **Date:** 2026-08-14
**Scope:** Single-purchase payments via Stripe (no subscriptions), free preview tier with blurred content, trip-credit accounting.

---

## 1. Summary

Travellin' stays free to try: anyone can generate an itinerary and see enough of it to want the rest. The full plan is paid. A **$5 purchase unlocks the current trip and includes one more full trip**; after those two trips are used, further generations are paywalled behind **$5 for 2 trips** or **$20 for 10**.

The mechanic is a **trip-credit ledger**, not a subscription. One "trip" = one generation batch (a destination + preferences, including all 3 theme variants and edits within it). All purchases go through Stripe Checkout (hosted page, one-time payments). Purchasing requires being signed in (Google).

The single most important technical constraint, confirmed in the codebase: **blur must not be cosmetic**. Generated content is persisted to the publicly readable `itinerary_jobs` table, mirrored into `localStorage`, and saved verbatim into `saved_trips`. Anything the user hasn't paid for must therefore **never be generated or delivered** — the blur sits over client-side placeholder text, and the real content doesn't exist until it's paid for. This also saves generation cost on every free preview.

## 2. Product spec

### 2.1 Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Trip (batch)** | One generation run: a destination + preferences, identified by the existing `batchId` (`Index.tsx:835-841`). Includes all 3 theme variants, retries, and edits. |
| **Credit** | The right to one full trip. Stored as a ledger of grants (+) and consumptions (−). |
| **Preview trip** | A trip generated free of charge with locked content (see 2.2). Available to users who have never purchased, including signed-out users. |
| **Entitled trip** | A trip a credit has been spent on. Generates in full, forever (retries and variant clicks within it are free). |
| **Purchaser** | A user with ≥1 purchase. Purchasers don't get preview trips: with credits they generate full trips; at zero credits, Generate is paywalled. |

### 2.2 The free preview

Free users (no purchase yet) can generate unlimited preview trips. What a preview contains:

**Variant 1 (auto-generated on "Generate trip"):**

- **Overview tab** — fully visible: summary hero, highlights, flights, budget, accommodation, alternatives, assumptions, sources. (This is the hook; it's also generated by the same model call that produces day titles, so hiding it wouldn't save money.)
- **Days tab** — days 1–3 fully generated and visible. Days 4+ show the **day title, location, and transit note** (from the plan skeleton) but their periods are never generated: the period selector and activity/dining area render blurred placeholder lines with a lock. Day pills for locked days carry a lock glyph; tapping one opens the unlock dialog.
- **Bookings tab** — the **"Book first"** group is fully visible. The "Book soon" and "Book anytime" section headers are visible, but all of their items are blurred placeholders with a lock. *(Confirmed.)*

**Variants 2 and 3 (generated on first click of their switcher pill):**

- **Overview tab** — generated and fully visible (research + plan skeleton run; day writing is skipped entirely).
- **Days tab** — every day shows title/location only; all periods locked and blurred.
- **Bookings tab** — entirely locked and blurred.

Trips of **3 days or fewer are effectively fully free** under this rule (day 4+ doesn't exist). Accepted for now; see §8.

**Edits:** the "edit itinerary" feature is a full-itinerary rewrite (a real cost center, currently unmetered). Edits are only available on entitled trips; on a preview trip the edit control opens the unlock dialog. Per-item feedback (thumbs up/down regeneration) stays available on *visible* items only — locked items have no real content to act on.

**Export/save:** preview trips can be saved and PDF-exported, but both operate on the redacted content, so they naturally exclude locked material.

### 2.3 The unlock dialog

Shown when a free user interacts with any locked element (locked day pill, blurred region CTA, locked bookings, edit on a preview trip).

- **Headline:** "Unlock your full trip" (gradient text treatment, matching the hero H1 style at `Index.tsx:1065`).
- **Value list:** ✓ Every day of this itinerary, fully planned · ✓ Complete booking checklist with links · ✓ All three trip styles, in full · ✓ **Plus one more full trip generation, on us**
- **CTA:** "Unlock for $5" → Stripe Checkout. Sub-copy: "One-time purchase. No subscription."
- If signed out: the CTA first opens the existing `AuthModal` (Google sign-in), reusing the pending-action queue pattern (`Index.tsx:511-544`) so checkout resumes right after the OAuth round-trip.

### 2.4 After purchase

On return from Stripe Checkout (success):

1. The app confirms the payment (see §5.4), credits land: **+2 credits, −1 immediately consumed to entitle the current trip** (net: 1 remaining).
2. All blur/lock UI on the current trip disappears; a background **resume generation** fills in the locked days and bookings for any variant that was already opened (see §4.4). Variants not yet opened generate in full on click, free.
3. A toast confirms: "Trip unlocked — 1 more full trip included."
4. The user menu shows remaining credits ("1 trip left").

The remaining credit lets the user generate one more trip, which generates **in full from the start** (no preview/unlock dance for paid trips).

### 2.5 The paywall (out of credits)

When a purchaser with 0 credits clicks "Generate trip", generation is blocked and a paywall modal appears (this is the "3rd trip" moment: $5 bought trips 1–2; trip 3 hits this).

Design intent: enticing and aesthetic, consistent with the app's design language (SF Pro display font, cyan-blue primary, `gradient-hero` background treatment, `shadow-medium` cards).

- **Headline:** "Where to next?" echoing the brand line, with sub-copy "You've used your trips. Keep exploring."
- **Two option cards side by side** (stacked on mobile):
  - **$5 — 2 trips** ($2.50/trip)
  - **$20 — 10 trips** ($2.00/trip) — tagged **"Best value"** with the primary-color badge
- Each card: price large, trips count, per-trip price small, full-width CTA. One-time purchase noted; Stripe handles the rest.
- Dismissible — the user keeps access to everything already generated/entitled.

Purchasers at 0 credits do **not** fall back to preview generation (explicit product decision; revisit if conversion data suggests otherwise).

### 2.6 Pricing & unit economics

Charged prices: **$5 → 2 credits**, **$20 → 10 credits**. The initial unlock is the same $5/2-credit product with one credit auto-spent on the current trip — one SKU fewer to maintain, and the "this trip + 1 more" promise falls out of the ledger math.

Cost reality (from the live `generation_costs` instrumentation, `_shared/costs.ts`): a full variant runs research (6–7 Perplexity calls, ~$0.03 in request fees alone) + a Sonnet plan call (~8k output tokens) + one Sonnet call per day. Preview gating saves the day-writing calls (the largest block for variants 2/3) but still pays for research + plan on every variant a user opens. Before launch, pull the real numbers:

```sql
select mode, count(*), round(avg(total_usd), 4) as avg_usd,
       round(avg(total_usd / nullif(day_count, 0)), 4) as avg_usd_per_day
from generation_costs
where function_name = 'generate-itinerary'
group by mode;
```

Expect roughly $0.35–$0.70 per full variant, $1–2 per fully-explored trip — meaning $2.00–2.50 revenue per trip is margin-positive but not by much if users open all variants and edit heavily. This is why edits are gated (§2.2) and why `mode` will distinguish preview from full runs in the cost table, so free-tier spend is observable from day one.

## 3. Current state (what the code does today)

Facts the design builds on, verified in the codebase:

1. **No edge function knows who's calling.** All functions have `verify_jwt = false` (`supabase/config.toml`), and the client sends only the anon `apikey` — `getHeaders()` at `Index.tsx:389-392` never attaches the user's access token. The session exists client-side but is only used for `saved_trips` and UI.
2. **Generation is per-variant, serialized, resumable.** "Generate trip" runs `suggest-themes` (3 themes, one cheap call), then generates **variant 1 only** (`Index.tsx:844`). Variants 2/3 have *nothing* until their pill is clicked (`handleSelectVariant`, `Index.tsx:741`), which triggers a full from-scratch generation. All variants of a trip share a `batchId`.
3. **The server already separates planning from writing.** `generate-itinerary` produces a plan skeleton (Sonnet tool call `emit_trip_plan`) containing **every day's title, location, transit note and dining roster** plus summary/budget/flights/accommodation/bookingChecklist — *before* any day prose is written. Day prose is then written in parallel slices (1 day per call for ≤10-day trips), assembled at `index.ts:2271-2313`. The gate slots exactly between these two phases.
4. **A "title but no content" day already exists.** Failed day slices emit `{dayNumber, title, location, transitNote, periods: [], generationFailed: true}` (`placeholderDay`, `index.ts:2256-2267`) and the client renders a notice (`StructuredItinerary.tsx:565-577`). The locked-day shape is this pattern with a `locked` flag.
5. **Content leaks everywhere by design.** The full emitted stream is persisted to `itinerary_jobs.content`, which has an "Anyone can read" RLS policy (recovery polling depends on it). Content is also snapshotted to `localStorage` and to `saved_trips.variants`. Hence: redact server-side, never client-side.
6. **The display surface is a 3-tab structured view** (`StructuredItinerary.tsx`): Overview / Days / Bookings. Days render one at a time behind day-selector pills — locking attaches naturally to pills and the day body, not to scrolled content. The bookings tab renders three priority groups in order: Book first, Book soon, Book anytime. There's an existing blur-overlay idiom to copy (`ItineraryOutput.tsx:1273-1282`) and a bespoke dialog to mirror (`AuthModal.tsx`).
7. **Cost tracking is live.** Every generation writes a row to `generation_costs` (deny-all RLS) with per-phase dollar totals — but no `user_id`, so per-customer margin needs a schema addition.
8. **Deploys:** frontend via Lovable; migrations + edge functions via GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/deploy-edge-functions.yml`) — note it deploys functions as hardcoded individual steps, so new functions need new steps.

## 4. Technical spec — data model & generation gating

### 4.1 New tables (one migration)

```sql
-- One row per completed Stripe checkout. The idempotency anchor.
create table purchases (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid not null references auth.users(id) on delete cascade,
  stripe_session_id text not null unique,        -- checkout.session.id
  stripe_payment_intent text,
  product_key text not null,                     -- 'pack_2' | 'pack_10'
  amount_cents int not null,
  credits int not null,
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

-- Append-only credit ledger. Balance = sum(delta).
create table credit_ledger (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid not null references auth.users(id) on delete cascade,
  delta int not null,                            -- +2, +10, -1
  reason text not null,                          -- 'purchase' | 'trip_entitlement' | 'refund' | 'grant'
  purchase_id uuid references purchases(id),
  batch_id uuid,                                 -- set when reason = 'trip_entitlement'
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);
create unique index credit_ledger_one_spend_per_batch
  on credit_ledger (batch_id) where reason = 'trip_entitlement';

-- Which trips are fully unlocked. The server-side gate checks this.
create table trip_entitlements (
  batch_id uuid primary key,
  user_id uuid not null references auth.users(id) on delete cascade,
  ledger_id uuid not null references credit_ledger(id),
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

-- Resume state for gated generations. Deny-all RLS; service role only.
-- Public itinerary_jobs.content holds only the redacted emitted stream.
create table itinerary_jobs_private (
  job_id uuid primary key,
  content text not null,        -- unredacted emitted JSON (skeleton + written days)
  resume_state jsonb,           -- {research, planFindings, preferences, themeVariant}
  updated_at timestamptz not null default now()
);
```

RLS: `purchases`, `credit_ledger`, `trip_entitlements` — owner `select` (`auth.uid() = user_id`), no client writes (service role only). `itinerary_jobs_private` — RLS enabled, zero policies (same pattern as `generation_costs`).

A `credit_balances` view (`security_invoker`) sums the ledger so the client can read its own balance with one query. Also in this migration: `alter table generation_costs add column user_id uuid;`, `alter table itinerary_jobs add column user_id uuid;` (attribution now, RLS tightening later — §7), and an owner UPDATE policy on `saved_trips` — today saves are insert-only, so "re-save after unlock" would duplicate the trip; the save path becomes an upsert keyed by trip id. Regenerate `src/integrations/supabase/types.ts` afterward (note: `generation_costs` is already missing from the generated types).

### 4.2 Atomic operations (Postgres functions, called with service role)

- **`apply_purchase(p_session_id, p_user, p_product_key, p_amount_cents, p_credits, p_unlock_batch uuid default null)`** — inserts the purchase (`on conflict (stripe_session_id) do nothing`; if conflict, return early — webhook retries and the confirm fallback make double-delivery normal), inserts the `+credits` ledger row, and if `p_unlock_batch` is present, spends one credit on it via `entitle_batch`. One transaction.
- **`entitle_batch(p_user, p_batch)`** — checks `sum(delta) >= 1` for the user, inserts the `-1` ledger row and the `trip_entitlements` row. The partial unique index makes a double-spend on the same batch a no-op instead of a second charge. Returns the new balance, or raises if balance is 0 (the caller then shows the paywall).

### 4.3 Gating inside `generate-itinerary`

**Auth plumbing (prerequisite):** `getHeaders()` becomes async and attaches `Authorization: Bearer <session.access_token>` when a session exists (anon key stays for signed-out users). `generate-itinerary` keeps `verify_jwt = false` (signed-out previews must still work) but resolves the caller manually via `supabase.auth.getUser(jwt)` when the header carries a user token. Resolved `user_id` is stamped onto `itinerary_jobs` and `generation_costs`.

**Tier resolution** (server-side, per request — nothing tier-relevant is trusted from the client):

```
entitled  = trip_entitlements has row for request.batchId
          — else, if caller is a purchaser with balance ≥ 1 and this is a new
            batch: entitle_batch() spends a credit now and entitled = true
          — else, if caller is a purchaser with balance 0: reject 402 (paywall)
role      = 'primary' if this jobId is the first/only job registered for the
            batch in itinerary_jobs, else 'secondary'   (server-derived; the
            client's variantRole field is a hint only)
tier      = entitled ? FULL : (role == 'primary' ? PREVIEW_PRIMARY : PREVIEW_SECONDARY)
```

The credit spend happens **here**, at the moment the server accepts the first generation of a new batch — not in any earlier "check" call. An abandoned click (themes failed, tab closed before the request) can never consume a credit, and once the batch is entitled, retries and other variants are free by construction (`entitle_batch` is idempotent per batch). A malicious client faking `variantRole` gains nothing: the server derives it, and even a spoofed extra preview is content it could get anyway by starting a new preview trip.

**The gate itself** sits at the seam the pipeline already has — after the plan skeleton (which produces all day titles + overview sections) and before day-slice writing (`index.ts:2119`, `const slices = planDaySlices(roster.length)`):

- `FULL` — today's behavior, untouched.
- `PREVIEW_PRIMARY` — slice only `roster.slice(0, 3)`; days 4+ are emitted as locked placeholders `{dayNumber, title, location, transitNote, periods: [], locked: true}` (the `placeholderDay` shape with `locked` instead of `generationFailed`). `bookingChecklist` is redacted: `high`-priority items intact; `medium`/`low` items replaced by `{count}` stubs so the client can render the right number of blurred rows without receiving names, costs, or URLs.
- `PREVIEW_SECONDARY` — no day slices at all; every day is a locked placeholder; `bookingChecklist` fully stubbed. Overview sections emit in full.

Emitted JSON gains a top-level `access` block: `{tier, lockedDayCount, lockedBookingCounts}` — the client renders lock UI purely from the response shape (no separate client feature flag; a server env var `PAYWALL=on|off` is the single kill switch, following the existing `ITINERARY_SPLIT_GENERATION` pattern).

**Persistence split:** the redacted stream goes to `itinerary_jobs.content` (public, unchanged recovery polling); `itinerary_jobs_private` gets the *unredacted* known content (full skeleton including the complete booking checklist, plus whatever days were written) **and the resume state**. The resume state matters because pass 2 doesn't run off the plan alone: every day-slice call feeds the raw research blocks into the prompt (`index.ts:2219-2222`). So a preview run must persist `{research blocks, plan-review findings, preferences, themeVariant}` alongside the skeleton — otherwise unlocking would have to re-run research (~30s + real cost) to write the remaining days. `finalizeCosts` records `mode: 'split_preview_primary' | 'split_preview_secondary'` so free-tier spend is visible in `generation_costs` immediately.

### 4.4 Resume-on-unlock

This is the one place the generation sequence itself changes shape. Today the pipeline is strictly one-shot: request reading → research → plan skeleton → critique → day slices → assemble, all in one invocation's memory, nothing intermediate persisted. Resume requires a **second entry point** that re-enters at the day-writing pass, which means two structural changes to `generate-itinerary`:

1. **Extract pass 2 into a callable unit.** The day-slice + assembly block (`index.ts:2119-2339`) becomes a function taking `{roster, skeleton, researchBlocks, planFindings, systemBlocks, daysToWrite, daysAlreadyWritten}` — invoked by the normal path with in-memory values, and by the resume path with values rehydrated from `itinerary_jobs_private.resume_state`. The system prompt is rebuilt deterministically from the stored preferences/themeVariant (it's a pure function of them today).
2. **Persist the intermediate state on preview runs** (§4.3) — skeleton, written days, research blocks, findings, preferences. This is new: nothing like it exists, and without it "generate the rest later" is impossible without paying for research twice.

The resume request itself: `{completeBatchId, jobId}` on the same function. Server verifies the JWT **and** `trip_entitlements` for the batch, loads the private row, runs day slices for **only the locked days**, merges them with the already-written days in trip order, and streams the completed JSON through the exact same SSE + `[DONE]` + `itinerary_jobs` polling contract as a normal generation — so the client's streaming, recovery, and parsing machinery is reused untouched. Both job rows are updated (public redacted → now full; private superseded) and costs record `mode: 'resume_unlock'`. Wall-clock is comfortable: resume skips research (~32s) and planning (~77s), running only day slices.

Fallback: if the private row is missing (legacy trip, purge), fall back to a full regeneration for that variant. Client-side, resume slots into the existing per-variant queue (`enqueueVariant`) so it serializes with any in-flight generation, and runs automatically for every already-opened variant after checkout success.

### 4.5 Spending a credit on a new trip

There is **no separate "begin trip" endpoint** — an earlier draft had one, and it created a real bug: consuming the credit before generation starts means an abandoned click (themes call fails, tab closes) burns a credit on a batch that never generated, and the next click mints a fresh batch and burns another.

Instead, responsibilities split cleanly:

- **Client (UX only):** the entitlements context already knows `hasPurchased` and `balance` from RLS-scoped reads. In `handleGenerate`, a purchaser at 0 credits gets the `PaywallModal` immediately — before theme suggestion, before any spinner. This is presentation, not enforcement.
- **Server (enforcement + spend):** `generate-itinerary` itself performs the spend as part of tier resolution (§4.3): first accepted generation of a new batch from a purchaser with balance → `entitle_batch` (atomic, advisory-locked per user); purchaser with 0 balance and un-entitled batch → **402**, which the client renders as the paywall (covers stale client state and any bypass attempt).

Because the spend entitles the **batch** and `entitle_batch` is idempotent per batch (partial unique index), retries and variants 2/3 of a paid trip never re-charge, and a failed generation costs no extra credit to retry. Two tabs racing with 1 credit resolve safely: one spends, the other finds the batch already entitled (same batch) or gets 402 (different batch).

**Failure policy (confirmed):** we eat the cost, never the user. A day slice that fails renders with a retry CTA (the existing failed-day pattern); retrying a variant, a failed resume, or a failed paid generation re-runs only the missing work under the already-entitled batch. No refund flow is needed because no failure path can consume a second credit.

## 5. Technical spec — Stripe

### 5.1 Stripe account objects

Two Products, each with a one-time USD Price (created in the Stripe dashboard; live + test mode):

| product_key | Price | Credits | Price ID env var |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pack_2` | $5.00 | 2 | `STRIPE_PRICE_PACK_2` |
| `pack_10` | $20.00 | 10 | `STRIPE_PRICE_PACK_10` |

Credits-per-pack lives in server code keyed by `product_key` — never trusted from the client. Stripe Checkout (hosted) means **no Stripe.js, no publishable key, no PCI surface in the frontend** — the client just follows a URL.

### 5.2 `create-checkout-session` (new edge function)

Auth required (manual `getUser`; 401 if absent — the UI ensures sign-in first). Body: `{pack: 'pack_2' | 'pack_10', unlockBatchId?: uuid}`.

Creates a Checkout Session: `mode: 'payment'`, the pack's Price ID, `customer_email` from the JWT (v1: no Customer reuse; Stripe dedupes receipts by email well enough), `client_reference_id: user_id`, `metadata: {user_id, product_key, unlock_batch_id?}`, `success_url: <origin>/?checkout=success&session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}`, `cancel_url: <origin>/?checkout=cancelled`. Returns `{url}`; client does `window.location.href = url`.

`unlockBatchId` is only accepted if that batch isn't already entitled. The origin for redirect URLs comes from an allowlist (env), not from the request.

### 5.3 `stripe-webhook` (new edge function)

`verify_jwt = false`; authenticates by verifying the `Stripe-Signature` header against `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (constructEvent). Handles:

- **`checkout.session.completed`** with `payment_status = 'paid'` → `apply_purchase(...)` from the session's metadata. Idempotent by `stripe_session_id` unique constraint; returns 200 on duplicate.
- **`checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` / `async_payment_failed`** → same handler / no-op. (Card-only checkout makes these rare, but handling them is cheap insurance.)
- **`charge.refunded`** → log + insert a compensating negative ledger row (`reason: 'refund'`) capped at the purchase's unspent credits; entitlements already consumed are not clawed back. Refunds themselves are issued manually in the Stripe dashboard for v1.

Everything else: 200 and ignore. Configure the endpoint in the Stripe dashboard pointing at `/functions/v1/stripe-webhook`.

### 5.4 Checkout return flow (client)

On load with `?checkout=success&session_id=...`:

1. Optimistically show a "Confirming your purchase…" state.
2. Poll the user's `credit_balances` / `trip_entitlements` (RLS-scoped selects) for up to ~15s waiting for the webhook to land.
3. **Fallback:** if nothing lands, call **`confirm-checkout`** (new edge function, auth required): it retrieves the session from Stripe by id, verifies `payment_status = 'paid'` and `metadata.user_id` matches the caller, then runs the same `apply_purchase`. This closes the "paid but webhook delayed/lost" gap deterministically; idempotency makes racing the webhook harmless.
4. On confirmation: toast, refresh entitlements context, kick off resume generation (§4.4) if `unlock_batch_id` was on the session.
5. `?checkout=cancelled`: quiet toast ("No charge — your trip is still here"), reopen nothing.

The pre-checkout app state survives the redirect via the existing `trvln:session:v1` snapshot; the pending-purchase intent (which batch, which dialog) rides the same localStorage pattern as `PENDING_SAVE_KEY`.

### 5.5 Config & secrets

- New env (Supabase function secrets): `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `STRIPE_PRICE_PACK_2`, `STRIPE_PRICE_PACK_10`, `CHECKOUT_ORIGIN_ALLOWLIST`, `PAYWALL`.
- `config.toml`: add `create-checkout-session`, `stripe-webhook`, `confirm-checkout` — all `verify_jwt = false` (auth is manual; webhook uses signatures).
- `deploy-edge-functions.yml`: add deploy steps for the three new functions (the workflow deploys per-function, hardcoded).
- Stripe SDK: `npm:stripe` (supported by the Supabase Edge runtime) — used for signature verification and session create/retrieve.

## 6. Technical spec — client

### 6.1 Entitlements state

`EntitlementsProvider` (sibling to `AuthProvider`): exposes `{balance, hasPurchased, entitledBatches: Set<uuid>, refresh()}` from two RLS-scoped selects when a session exists; all-defaults when signed out. Refreshed on auth change, on checkout return, and after `begin-trip`. `UserMenu` gains a "N trips left" line for purchasers.

### 6.2 Locked-content UI

All lock rendering is driven by the response shape (`locked: true` days, stubbed booking items, the `access` block) — never by client-side tier logic.

- **`LockedPanel`** — the reusable blur unit, copying the existing overlay idiom (`ItineraryOutput.tsx:1273-1282`): relative wrapper, static placeholder lines (skeleton bars via the existing `skeleton`/unused `animate-shimmer` utilities — *not* real text), `select-none pointer-events-none`, an `absolute inset-0 backdrop-blur` overlay, and a centered chip: lock icon + "Unlock the full trip" → opens `UnlockDialog`.
- **Days tab** (`StructuredItinerary.tsx`): locked day pills get a lock glyph; selecting one renders the day header card normally (title/location tease) with `LockedPanel` in place of the period selector + activities.
- **Bookings tab**: "Book first" renders normally; "Book soon" + "Book anytime" render group headers with `LockedPanel` rows sized by the server's stub counts.
- **Variant pills**: no lock on pills (variants 2/3 are clickable — they generate their free overview); their Days/Bookings tabs show locks per the response.
- **`UnlockDialog`** — mirrors `AuthModal`'s structure (per §2.3 copy). CTA: sign-in if needed → `create-checkout-session` → redirect.
- **`PaywallModal`** — the two-pack modal (§2.5), shown when `begin-trip` returns `paywalled`. Larger than the dialog (`sm:max-w-lg`), `gradient-hero`-style backdrop, two `shadow-medium` cards, "Best value" badge on `pack_10`.
- **Edit control** on preview trips: opens `UnlockDialog` instead of the edit panel.
- **Markdown fallback path** (`ItineraryOutput.tsx` legacy renderer): only reachable for entitled trips in practice (it renders post-edit content, and edits are gated), but the day-grouping renderer gets a guard that wraps locked days in `LockedPanel` for safety.

### 6.3 `handleGenerate` changes

No client-side paywall pre-check (revised during implementation: the client can't see the server's `PAYWALL` switch, and a pre-check would block paying users while the kill switch is off). The server resolves tier and performs any credit spend itself (§4.5); a 402 from `generate-itinerary` routes to the `PaywallModal`, costing one cheap themes call for an out-of-credits purchaser.

## 7. Hardening & follow-ons (in scope, phased)

- **`itinerary_jobs` scoping**: done in the foundation migration (pulled forward from this phase during implementation review — paid content lands in this table, so world-readability was an active bypass vector, not deferred hardening). Reads are owner-scoped; anonymous rows (always redacted preview content) stay open so signed-out recovery polling works. Entitlement checks in `generate-itinerary` are additionally caller-scoped, and `entitle_batch` refuses cross-user idempotency.
- **Abuse limits on free previews**: previews still cost real money (research + skeleton ≈ $0.15–0.35 per opened variant). Add a coarse per-IP/day cap on `generate-itinerary` preview-tier requests (mirroring `send-ready-text`'s in-memory limiter note) before any marketing push.
- **`edit-itinerary` metering**: it gains auth + entitlement check in Phase 1 (gate), and cost tracking (it currently records nothing).
- **Stripe Tax**: off for v1 (US-only, low volume). Revisit before real volume; enabling `automatic_tax` later is additive.
- **Receipts/records**: Stripe emails receipts; no in-app purchase history page in v1 (the data model supports one).

## 8. Decisions (confirmed 2026-08-14)

1. **Bookings blur boundary** — only "Book first" is visible; "Book soon" and "Book anytime" contents are blurred (group headers visible).
2. **Variants 2/3 lock all days for free users** — days 1–3 are visible only on variant 1. (Trips ≤3 days come out fully free on variant 1 by construction; accepted.)
3. **No free previews at 0 credits** — a purchaser out of credits is blocked at Generate; paywall shows.
4. **Failures are on the house** — every failure path gets a retry CTA; retries re-run only the missing work under the already-entitled batch and can never consume a second credit (§4.5).
5. **Sign-in triggers** — at purchase, plus the existing trigger points (save trip, view saved trips). Anonymous preview generation stays open.
6. **USD only** for v1.

## 9. Build plan

Phases are sequential; each is releasable behind the `PAYWALL` env flag (off = today's behavior, bit-for-bit).

**Phase 0 — Plumbing (no user-visible change)**
Migration (§4.1) + Postgres functions (§4.2) + regenerate DB types. Async `getHeaders()` with JWT. `generate-itinerary` resolves `user_id`, stamps jobs + costs. Deploy-workflow steps for upcoming functions.
*Accept:* signed-in generations produce `itinerary_jobs.user_id` / `generation_costs.user_id`; signed-out generation unchanged.

**Phase 1 — Preview gating (server truncation + locked UI)**
Tier resolution + gate + redaction + `itinerary_jobs_private` split (§4.3). `variantRole` from client. `LockedPanel`, locked day pills, bookings redaction UI, `UnlockDialog` (CTA disabled with "coming soon" tooltip until Phase 2 in staging; the two phases ship to production together). Edit gating. Cost `mode` values.
*Accept:* with `PAYWALL=on`, a fresh trip shows days 1–3 + locked 4+, redacted bookings; variants 2/3 overview-only; no locked content present in job rows, localStorage, saved trips, or PDF export; with `PAYWALL=off`, today's behavior.

**Phase 2 — Stripe (unlock path end-to-end)**
Products/Prices in Stripe (test mode), `create-checkout-session`, `stripe-webhook`, `confirm-checkout`, checkout return flow, `apply_purchase`/`entitle_batch` wiring, resume-on-unlock (§4.4), entitlements context, credits in UserMenu.
*Accept:* test-mode purchase from the unlock dialog → blur gone, locked days stream in via resume, balance shows 1; replaying the webhook or hitting confirm twice grants nothing extra; refund event docks unspent credits.

**Phase 3 — Credit spend + paywall**
Purchaser tier resolution + credit spend inside `generate-itinerary`, client pre-check, `PaywallModal`, `pack_10`.
*Accept:* purchaser's next trip consumes 1 credit and generates fully (all variants, no locks); at 0 credits Generate opens the paywall and nothing generates; buying either pack from the paywall proceeds straight into full generation.

**Phase 4 — Hardening**
§7 items: preview rate limiting, `edit-itinerary` cost tracking, `itinerary_jobs` RLS tightening, live-mode Stripe keys + webhook endpoint, launch checklist (test cards through real UI on production infra, cost dashboard query saved).

**Testing throughout:** Stripe test cards + `stripe listen --forward-to` against a local `supabase functions serve`; webhook idempotency (deliberate double-send); ledger invariants (balance never negative — enforced in `entitle_batch`); RLS checks (user A cannot read user B's ledger/purchases; anon cannot read `itinerary_jobs_private`); the four preview/full/resume/paywalled generation paths against a seeded user.
